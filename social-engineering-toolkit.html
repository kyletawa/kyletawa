Understand. Recognize. Resist.

Social engineering attacks exploit human psychology — not technical vulnerabilities. This toolkit covers the most common manipulation techniques used by attackers, with real-world scenarios, red flags, and actionable prevention tips. Use this as a training reference for security awareness programs.

🚪 Tailgating & Physical Access

3 scenarios

Tailgating (or "piggybacking") relies on social courtesy to bypass physical security controls. Attackers follow authorized personnel through secured doors, turnstiles, or checkpoints.

🧠 Awareness Training Tips

8 tips

Build a security-minded culture. These actionable tips help individuals and teams recognize and resist social engineering attempts.

🔑 Key Principle: When in doubt, verify through a separate, trusted channel. If an email from "IT" asks for your password, pick up the phone and call the IT department directly. Trust, but verify.

📋 Quick Reference

Attack types at a glance
Attack Type Human Vulnerability Common Vector Best Defense
Phishing Curiosity, urgency, fear Email, SMS, chat, voice Verify senders, don't click suspicious links
Spear Phishing Trust in known contacts Targeted email with personal details Confirm unusual requests offline
Whaling Respect for authority Email impersonating executives Multi-factor authentication, clear approval workflows
Vishing Trust in phone calls Phone call, VoIP spoofing Hang up and call back on official number
Smishing Immediacy of text messages SMS with malicious links Don't click links in unsolicited texts
Pretexting Trust in a fabricated identity In-person, phone, email Verify identity through independent channels
Baiting Greed, curiosity, convenience USB drops, fake downloads, free offers Never plug unknown devices; use official sources
Tailgating Politeness, conflict avoidance Physical proximity to secured entrances Challenge strangers; use mantraps & badges
Quid Pro Quo Reciprocity, desire for help Fake tech support, fake surveys Verify the caller; never grant access unsolicited
Watering Hole Trust in familiar websites Compromised legitimate sites Keep software updated; use endpoint protection
